Day-Ahead Imbalance Price Forecasting: What Was Tested and What It Shows

Recurrent encoders and market-rule blocks in a 96-horizon MRINN extension

30 August 2026 Test window 2025 9 models · 4 rolling folds 34,521 origins 7.7 h CPU

Nine forecasting models were trained and evaluated under one identical protocol to answer two questions the project had left open: does a recurrent encoder beat MRINN's Dense encoder at a day-ahead horizon, and do the differentiable market-rule blocks contribute anything measurable at all. Four defects in the prior evaluation were found and corrected first, without which no comparison would have been valid. The recurrent-encoder claim is confirmed and now statistically significant; the market-rule claim survives only in combination with the GRU encoder, and is null elsewhere. Section 5 assesses five supervisor suggestions against the same data, and records which hold, which need correction, and which are blocked.

1What was tested

Task and data

Austrian/German balancing-market imbalance price at 15-minute resolution (imbalance_data.csv, 140,249 rows, 2022–2026). At each origin the model predicts all 96 horizons — 15 minutes to 24 hours — in a single forward pass. 17 input signals; no lagged price among them.

Protocol

Rolling 12-month training windows retrained quarterly, four folds covering all of 2025. Per-fold scalers, inverted per fold before pooling. T = 32 (8 h history), stride 2 on training rows only; batch 256, constant LR with plateau decay, early stopping on validation loss. Single seed (42).

The nine models

The design is a 2×3 grid plus references. Every model shares the data pipeline, horizon embedding, known-future covariate path, day-ahead outage dropout and — except LQR and XGBoost — the hierarchical quantile head. Reading down a column isolates the encoder; reading across a row isolates the market-rule blocks.

Metrics

AQL (average pinball loss over q = 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90) is the headline; MAE and RMSE of the median; AQCR (quantile-crossing rate); 80% interval coverage. Reported at h = 1 (15 min), h = 4 (1 hour) and h = 96 (1 day), and averaged over all 96.

On the leak's severity

The leaked price is genuinely informative (a legitimate same-clock-time substitute explains only R² = 0.26 of it), but it barely predicts the target: univariate R² = 0.045 against 0.011 for the substitute. Closing it was necessary for validity and did not overturn the ranking.

4Conclusions

  1. The recurrent-encoder claim holds, and is now significant. MR-GRU beats MRINN by 0.245 AQL (p = 0.0017 on non-overlapping origins), wins all four folds, and is the best model at both 1 hour and 1 day — on 5,055 parameters, fewer than any other neural model tested. This overturns the project's own earlier conclusion of a tie at day-ahead, which was recorded before the leak and convergence defects were found.
  2. The market-rule blocks are worth far less than assumed. They are statistically null at the Dense and LSTM encoders and significant only with the GRU. Averaged across encoders they buy 0.53% of AQL. Their defensible value is parameter efficiency, not accuracy: MR-GRU matches or beats every rules-free model on 20% fewer parameters.
  3. GRU and LSTM are not interchangeable here. MR-GRU beats MR-LSTM significantly on both tests (p = 0.0014), so the cell type is a real choice rather than a matter of taste — and the cheaper cell is the better one.
  4. Gradient boosting wins the short horizon outright. XGBoost is best of all nine at 15 minutes (AQL 23.01 against MR-GRU's 24.96, a 7.8% margin) and worst at 24 hours. It exploits recent lags but has no horizon structure, and needs 96 separate boosters. Any claim of a single model for the whole day must concede this point.
  5. Zero quantile crossing is a property of the head, not of the encoder. Every model carrying the hierarchical quantile head scores AQCR = 0. The two models without it do not: XGBoost 2.75, LQR 18.07. This was previously inherited from the source paper as an assumption; it is now demonstrated.
  6. The honest headline is smaller than the one previously reported. Against climatology on matched rows, the best model is −12.3% AQL and −13.4% MAE — not −32% against seasonal naive, a baseline that itself loses to predicting a constant. RMSE sits at parity with a constant predictor for every model: these models forecast the calm bulk of the distribution and capture essentially none of the spike variance.

S1 — Netting into P_RE is the right block, but its influence is shrinking

Modifying P_RE is correct: it is decisive on the majority of intervals while P_SC is nearly inert. Placing netting upstream of the rule rather than adding a fourth parallel component is also correct — netting changes the volume requiring activation, it does not itself set a price.

Observation. Over the whole record P_RE decides 68.9% of intervals, but on the year actually tested it decides 53.8%. A netting improvement will therefore show on roughly half the test intervals, not two-thirds, and the write-up should quote the 2025 figure rather than the flattering whole-record one. Two checks before committing: system_imbalance is already 83.1% explained by net activation alone (r = +0.911), so INC may add little; and inside the model these signals are 8-dimensional learned encodings, so the block is netting-structured, not literal netting, and should not be over-claimed as physical fidelity.

S2 — The block diagram contradicts the code

The diagram shows a series chain. The implementation computes the three components in parallel and blends the smooth minimum and maximum through a learned gate; the only real dependency is P_EX → P_SC, because scarcity consumes P_base from the market-reference block.

Conclusion. Redraw before publication — a reviewer who opens the code will catch it. This is not cosmetic: the series diagram implies that netting entered at RE would propagate through EX and SC. It will not. Netting changes one of three parallel candidates and must then win the min/max gate to move the price at all, which is precisely why the declining P_RE share above matters.

S3 — Why sMAPE does not survive this price series

sMAPE divides by (|actual| + |forecast|) / 2, a denominator that is only well-behaved when prices are comfortably positive. Imbalance prices are not.

Conclusion. The headline would track the price level rather than forecast skill, driven by the 12.45% of intervals near or below zero. There is also a purpose mismatch: sMAPE exists to compare forecasts across series of different scales, and there is one series here, so scale-invariance buys nothing while costing interpretability. Propose MASE instead — scaled by in-sample seasonal-naive error, it handles zeros and negatives and is standard in the forecasting literature. If sMAPE is still required, report it restricted to |actual| > 10 alongside the unrestricted figure; the gap between the two is itself the argument.

S4 — The convergence complaint was correct

He was right, and the evidence was already in the project's own output: with a ceiling of 120 epochs and patience 12, every run stopped at 119 or 120. Patience never fired, so training ended because the loop ran out rather than because the model stopped improving.

Conclusion. The diagnosis was right but the lever was batch size, not the epoch number: what a model learns from is gradient updates, and epochs only buy updates at a rate the batch size sets. His 200 would have sufficed but only just — the deepest fold needed 109 epochs plus confirmation. Every model in this report now stops via early stopping. Stated honestly, the old runs were not badly wrong — validation loss improved only 0.4% from epoch 20 to 49 — they were unconverged and therefore indefensible, which is a more precise complaint and a more defensible fix.

S5 — What “INC” refers to is genuinely ambiguous

His own note that the signs need verifying is well placed. In a German control-zone context INC plausibly denotes either the IGCC imbalance-netting volume — MW of opposing imbalance cancelled against neighbouring TSOs before activation — or the reBAP incentive component (Anreizkomponente), which is a price adder, not a volume. Different quantities, different units, different sign conventions.

Conclusion. Building against the wrong one yields a model that trains, converges and means nothing. This is the only item that cannot be resolved without him, and it is compounded by an unresolved contradiction in the project's own records: the dataset is described as the Austrian balancing market in the project log and as German control-zone in the audit. The bidding zone should be confirmed at the same time as the sign convention.

6Limitations

Stated plainly
  • Single seed. Prior work measured seed noise at ±0.11–0.26 AQL, which exceeds several differences reported here. The DM tests are across origins, not seeds, so they do not cover seed variation.
  • No spike skill. RMSE parity with a constant is a real limitation, not a presentational one.
Not yet resolved
  • The dataset is described inconsistently as Austrian and as German control-zone. This does not affect the results above, but it must be settled before the proposed imbalance-netting extension, which depends on which market's INC series is meant.
  • Rule-component dominance is shifting: P_RE fell from 82.4% of intervals in 2022 to 53.8% in 2025 while P_EX rose to 44.7%.

7Recommended next steps

  1. Repeat at two further seeds for the four models that carry a claim (MR-GRU, MR-LSTM, MRINN, GRU). This is the single highest-value remaining run and the only way to convert "significant across origins" into "robust".
  2. Report the rules as an efficiency result, not an accuracy one, and state the encoder-dependence explicitly rather than averaging it away.
  3. Settle the market and the INC definition before building the netting block, and quote the 2025 P_RE share (53.8%) rather than the whole-record 68.9%.
  4. Replace sMAPE with MASE (§5, S3), and settle the bidding zone and INC definition (§5, S5) before any netting work begins.
